How it works

belsim is a small simulator of Belgium's public finances. You change a tax rate, a pension age or a spending rule, and it shows what happens to the deficit, the debt and employment up to . It is a toy to play with and argue over, not an official costing.

Figures marked like this are computed by the model when the page loads.

What it is, and what it is not

The model moves forward one year at a time. It follows the population, jobs and wages, the economy, and every revenue and spending line of the federal state, social security, the Communities and Regions, and local authorities. A measure is a change to one or more parameters from a given year. Its effect is the difference with "no policy change".

No public institution made, checked or endorsed it. It does not forecast anything, and its results are rough sizes: "one to two billion", not "1.5 rather than 1.4". For anything that matters, use the publications of the Federal Planning Bureau, the National Bank or the Court of Audit.

What is real, what is assumed

The starting point is real: the 2024 and 2025 public accounts, official statistics on population, jobs and incomes, and the tax and benefit rules in force today.

The rest is modelling. The model has parameters, each with its source. of them have no official source: mostly how people and firms react, and long-run trends. They are flagged as assumptions in the app, and you can change them.

The starting line: no policy change

Every result is compared with a baseline in which nothing new is decided. Five choices shape it:

  • Reforms already voted are in. Announced ones are out.
  • Up to 2031 it follows the Federal Planning Bureau's outlook; after that, the Study Committee on Ageing for pensions and health care.
  • The 2025 accounts are known, so they are kept as they are.
  • After 2030, tax thresholds follow wages rather than prices, as long-term projections usually assume.
  • Fuel and tobacco sales keep falling, by assumption.

The debt that keeps rising in this baseline is not a prediction. It is what happens on paper if nobody does anything.

Reading a result

Each scenario is computed twice. The static result changes the rule and assumes nobody reacts. The behavioural result lets people and firms react: work, hiring, wages, prices, spending.

The shaded band around a line shows the result with reactions half as strong and one and a half times as strong. It tells you how much the answer depends on behaviour. It is not a margin of error.

How close it gets

The model is checked against official figures on what measures cost or bring in. land within 10% of the official figure, within 25% and within 50%. The baseline is closer, but that is no merit: it follows the official projections.

Where it is weak

  • How people and firms react rests on a few coefficients tuned by hand, not on proper estimates.
  • Some results are far from the official ones, such as the corporate tax cut or the budget effect of a VAT rise after a few years.
  • Where the Planning Bureau and the National Bank disagree, the model follows the Bureau.
  • Revenue up to 2031 follows the Planning Bureau's path, not the model's own calculation.
  • Some recent reforms are missing, such as the capped indexation of high pensions and the new unemployment benefit scale.
  • Nobody independent has checked it. There are certainly errors left.

The full list is in docs/DETAILS.md.

Party programmes

The app can load the 2024 programme of each of parties, as costed by the Federal Planning Bureau, with the same method for all of them. The results are this model's and differ from the Bureau's, sometimes a lot, especially on employment. These are the proposals of 2024, not what the parties say today.

Households and regions

Households. The "who gains, who loses" view uses a few hundred typical households built from statistics, not real families from a survey. Look at the change a measure brings, not at the income levels.

Regions. Results are split between Flanders, Wallonia and Brussels. Transfers between regions depend on how federal revenue and spending are shared out; other reasonable rules give other figures.

How it was built

belsim was built in a few days in November 2026 by one person directing AI coding agents (Anthropic's Claude). The agents also fetched the data and wrote the French and Dutch texts, which are machine translations. Nobody independent has reviewed the model yet, so expect mistakes, and please report them.

Sources
